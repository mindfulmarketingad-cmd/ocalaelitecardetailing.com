Does Mobile Car Detailing Include Engine Cleaning?

By Ivan · August 5, 2026 · 6 min read · What Is Included

Book OnlineCall (757) 743-9050

In This Article

  1. Why It Is Normally Excluded
  2. When It Is Actually Included
  3. How It Is Done Safely On A Mobile Appointment
  4. Is It Worth Paying For Separately?

The short answer is that engine cleaning is usually not included in a standard mobile detail, and any detailer telling you otherwise is either quietly skipping it or charging for it somewhere in the package. It is a separate job with separate risk, and it is priced that way almost everywhere.

Why It Is Normally Excluded

Detailing packages are built around predictable labour. Wash, decontaminate, protect, vacuum, extract: those steps take a known amount of time on a known set of surfaces. An engine bay is not predictable. One vehicle needs twenty minutes; the next has a decade of baked-on oil film, a shredded intake liner, and a mouse nest.

The bigger reason is liability. Everything under the bonnet that matters electrically is exactly what water damages: alternator, fuse box, coil packs, exposed connectors, and on newer vehicles a genuinely alarming number of sensors. A detailer who soaks a bay carelessly can hand back a car that starts fine and develops an intermittent fault a week later, which is a conversation nobody wants.

Everything sensitive is covered before any liquid goes near the bay.

When It Is Actually Included

On our menu it is part of the full package, because that service is already a full working day and the bay fits into the sequence naturally. It is not part of exterior detailing or interior detailing, and we do not pretend otherwise in the package description.

Booked on its own it is engine detailing, which runs about an hour to two depending on what is under there.

How It Is Done Safely On A Mobile Appointment

  • Inspection first, so anything already damaged or modified is identified before water appears
  • Alternator, fuse box, intake, and exposed connectors covered
  • Low, controlled pressure only, never a pressure washer
  • Degreaser given dwell time, then agitated by hand rather than blasted off
  • Dried with compressed air and microfiber instead of being left to air dry
  • Rubber and plastic conditioned afterwards so they do not grey out again immediately

Mobile makes no difference to any of that. The same covering, the same low pressure, the same drying. What mobile does change is that the runoff has to be handled responsibly on your property, which is a reason to ask any detailer what they do with it.

Is It Worth Paying For Separately?

Two situations where it clearly is. Selling the vehicle, because opening a clean bay materially changes how a buyer reads the whole car. And diagnostics, because grime hides leaks, and a clean bay means a weeping gasket shows up as a fresh mark rather than blending into ten years of film.

If neither applies and the car is a daily driver you intend to keep, it is a reasonable thing to skip. We would rather say that than sell it to everyone.
